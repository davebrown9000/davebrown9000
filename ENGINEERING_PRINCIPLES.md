# Engineering Principles

I build data and AI-assisted systems around a simple principle: useful outputs must be supported by evidence, tested through reproducible controls, and understandable to the people responsible for them.

## Evidence before output

Separate source evidence from interpretation and generated content. Maintain traceable references, make assumptions visible, and distinguish verified information from proposed conclusions. Where AI helps organise or draft evidence, factual approval remains a deliberate step.

## AI with defined boundaries

Use AI for tasks such as requirements interpretation, analysis, drafting, and structuring information. Define the inputs, expected intermediate artefacts, constraints, and human decisions around those tasks. Treat model output as a candidate for evaluation, not proof of correctness.

## Deterministic quality gates

Apply programmatic checks where rules can be expressed precisely: schema validity, referential integrity, missing data, approval status, and output structure. Design failures to be visible and actionable rather than silently accepted.

## Continuous integration and reproducibility

Prefer version-controlled inputs, automated regression tests, repeatable execution, and documented build assumptions. CI provides evidence that the implemented checks behave consistently; it does not replace independent assessment of factual accuracy or system suitability.

## Human accountability

Keep meaningful review points where correctness, context, presentation, or publication cannot be determined by software alone. Record what was checked and distinguish automated validation from human authorisation.

## Responsible disclosure

Show engineering capability through clear problem statements, architecture, technical trade-offs, synthetic examples, and compact demonstrations. Keep original reusable implementations and non-public data private. Public examples are deliberately scoped to explain an approach without exposing an entire operational system.

## Portfolio scope

The public repositories associated with this profile are selective technical demonstrations and case studies. Each repository identifies the specific capability it demonstrates and its limitations. A passing test suite or a convincing example is not presented as evidence of capabilities that the published implementation does not include.
